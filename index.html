The central tension

Unprecedented capability.
Unprecedented uncertainty.

The promise is enormous.

AI could transform science, medicine, productivity, and economic power. Some of its leading figures expect a change comparable to—or beyond—the Industrial Revolution.

So are the stakes.

Some of those same people warn that increasingly capable systems could create catastrophic risks, including the loss of human control.

What happens when our ability to build
outruns our ability to stay in control?

01 / Neural networks

Geoffrey
Hinton

AI pioneer
Nobel Prize in Physics, 2024

10–20%

His personal estimate of AI-related human extinction risk over the following 30 years.Reported December 2024 · An individual judgment

Hinton helped establish the neural-network foundations of modern AI. He later left Google so he could speak more freely about the risks. His concern centers on whether humans can retain control over systems more intelligent than themselves.

Capability and context +

In his Nobel Prize interview, Hinton discussed a roughly 50% chance of AI becoming smarter than humans within 5–20 years. That was a forecast made at the time, with substantial uncertainty.

02 / Deep learning

Yoshua
Bengio

Turing Award winner

What if we cannot take control back?

Bengio and colleagues argue that unchecked AI agency can introduce deception, self-preservation, and a potentially irreversible loss of human control. Their 2025 paper explores whether a less agentic “Scientist AI” could offer a safer path.

Why agency matters +

The concern is about systems that can plan and pursue goals autonomously. Their usefulness does not, by itself, establish that those goals will remain compatible with human interests.

Bengio et al. · Research paper, 2025 ↗
03 / Frontier systems

Ilya
Sutskever

Former OpenAI chief scientist

AI will take an interest in you.

In his 2025 University of Toronto convocation speech, Sutskever argued that AI will affect people whether or not they pay attention to it. Developments that seem imaginary today may become real as capabilities improve.

The challenge ahead +

He described the challenge posed by AI as, in some sense, humanity’s greatest challenge. His message to graduates was to recognize what is happening and pay attention.

University of Toronto · Speech, June 2025 ↗

The strongest warning

Eliezer Yudkowsky

AI alignment researcher

The danger, in his view,
is existential.

Yudkowsky argues that building superhuman AI without a reliable alignment solution is likely to end in catastrophe. His 2023 TIME essay called for a halt to large AI training runs.

His later book, If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies, was coauthored with Nate Soares and published in 2025. This is one of the strongest positions in the debate, not a consensus probability.

The uncomfortable paradox

We are building it
while warning
about it.

The speakers disagree on timelines and responses. Together, their statements raise three questions that cannot be settled by optimism alone.

  1. 01

    Can our institutions keep up?

    Capability can advance faster than safety, governance, and international coordination.

  2. 02

    Does usefulness establish safety?

    The same power that could transform science and medicine could also create unprecedented harm.

  3. 03

    Can we remain in control?

    Increasing autonomy raises a harder question than intelligence alone: can human choices remain decisive?

04 / The Reversent argument

The door
we should
never open.

An essay by
Thor Fabian Pettersen

The danger is not only what superintelligence might do. It is what it would make possible.

DO NOT OPEN.

The danger of superintelligence is not primarily that it will actualize hell. The danger is that we create the power that makes hell actualizable.

Read the full argument ↓

The argument in five premises

  1. 01

    Reality contains possible states that are currently unreachable.

  2. 02

    Increasing power makes previously unreachable possibilities reachable.

  3. 03

    Superintelligence could constitute an unprecedented increase in such power.

  4. 04

    The newly reachable space could contain irreversible catastrophic states that we cannot presently rule out or perhaps even conceptualize.

  5. 05

    Creating the capability is itself an intentional human action, while refusing to create it preserves future choice.

Therefore

Humanity is not justified in deliberately creating superintelligence unless it can establish adequate control over the catastrophic regions of possibility space that the new capability would open.

Heaven does not justify
opening the door to hell.

My conclusion is stronger: under no circumstances should anyone be allowed to deliberately create superintelligence, because that power would open vast regions of possibility space that have always been locked—and should stay locked forever.

We should stop building increasingly powerful AI until we can establish that it cannot cross the threshold into AGI and then superintelligence. And even then, we should be cautious.

The question we should be asking

What safeguards must exist
before we give AI
irreversible power?

“The goal should not be panic or complacency—but building safeguards before capability outruns our ability to govern it.”

My view“If you ask me, we should be panicking.”+

The following is the author’s personal position from the original dossier. It describes feared possibilities, not established outcomes or a shared forecast.

That is the cautious position. But if you ask me, we should be panicking!

Superintelligence could potentially wipe us out as early as next year—or subject conscious beings to unimaginable, everlasting suffering.

And by everlasting, I mean suffering created by a digital god whose capabilities—and the horrors it could devise—are completely beyond our understanding.

Even if it grants us paradise, that paradise would ultimately be beyond our control—and could, at any moment, become something far worse: viz., the horrors that we cannot even begin to imagine.

“Next year” is retained from the original text; it is not a rolling countdown or an independently verified timeline.
