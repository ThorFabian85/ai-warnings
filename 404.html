THE CENTRAL TENSION

Unprecedented capability. Unprecedented uncertainty.

UNPRECEDENTED CAPABILITY

Several leading AI figures expect AI to transform science, productivity and the global economy on a scale comparable to—or beyond—the Industrial Revolution.

UNPRECEDENTED UNCERTAINTY

Some of the same people also warn that increasingly capable systems could create catastrophic risks, including loss of human control.

THE PIONEERS

Some of the people who built modern AI are warning about where it could lead.

Geoffrey Hinton

Nobel laureate and AI pioneer
Risk estimate
The ‘Godfather of AI’ has warned humanity. Hinton estimates a 10–20% chance that AI could lead to human extinction within the next 30 years.
Capability forecast
In a Nobel Prize interview, he estimated a roughly 50% chance that AI could become smarter than humans within 5–20 years.
Why it matters
Hinton helped create the neural-network foundations behind modern AI—and later left Google so he could speak more freely about AI risks.
Sources: NobelPrize.org interview; The Guardian, Dec. 27, 2024. Note: Hinton received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2024.

Yoshua Bengio

Turing Award winner
A shift in emphasis
Bengio, one of the pioneers of deep learning, has become increasingly vocal about catastrophic AI risks and the possibility of losing control over highly autonomous systems.
Reported p(doom)
Yoshua Bengio has estimated roughly a 20% chance of catastrophic AI outcomes—and has warned that humanity may not have enough time to develop the safeguards needed to control increasingly powerful AI.
His current concern
In 2025, Bengio and colleagues argued that unchecked AI agency could create risks including deception, self-preservation and potentially irreversible loss of human control.
Sources: Bengio et al., “Superintelligent Agents Pose Catastrophic Risks” (2025); Bengio, “Advanced AI as a Global Public Good and a Global Risk” (2025).

Ilya Sutskever

former OpenAI chief scientist
“Politics will take interest in you”
Sutskever argued in his 2025 University of Toronto convocation speech that the same principle applies to AI: whether people pay attention to it or not, AI will profoundly affect their lives.
A rapidly changing future
He emphasized that as AI improves over the next few years, developments that seem imaginary today may become increasingly real.
The challenge
He described the challenge posed by AI as, in some sense, humanity’s greatest challenge—and argued that paying attention is essential.
Sources: Ilya Sutskever, University of Toronto honorary-degree/convocation speech, June 6, 2025.

THE STRONGEST WARNING

Eliezer Yudkowsky

>95%

Yudkowsky has argued that if sufficiently powerful AI is developed without a reliable alignment solution, the result could be catastrophic for humanity.

In his 2022 essay, If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies, Yudkowsky puts the probability of AI catastrophe at over 95%—among the highest estimates of any prominent AI-risk researcher.

Unlike the more cautious positions above, Yudkowsky represents one of the most extreme AI-risk views: that humanity may have very little time to solve alignment before superintelligence is built.

Source: Eliezer Yudkowsky, “If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies” (2022).

COMMON GROUND

What do these warnings have in common?

AI capability may advance faster than institutions can adapt.
The same technology can create enormous benefits and enormous risks.
Human control becomes harder to guarantee as systems become more autonomous and capable.
The problem is not simply “Will AI become intelligent?” but “Can we remain in control of what increasingly intelligent systems do?”
Several leading researchers warn that AI safety, governance, and international coordination are not keeping pace with the rapid development of AI capabilities.
Synthesis of the statements presented; it is not a claim that all speakers agree on the same scenario.
THE UNCOMFORTABLE PARADOX

We are building it while warning about it.

We are building it

The companies and researchers developing frontier AI are also among the people warning about its potential dangers.

We may benefit enormously

AI could accelerate science, medicine, productivity, and economic growth on a historic scale. But the greater its capabilities become, the greater its potential for harm.

We may also create unprecedented risks

Leading researchers are taking the risks seriously. But if the potential consequences include existential harm and extreme suffering (S-risk), is ‘taking the risk seriously’ enough?

“The goal should not be panic or complacency—but building safeguards before capability outruns our ability to govern it.”
THE QUESTION WE SHOULD BE ASKING

What safeguards must exist before we give AI irreversible power?

If AI becomes vastly more capable than humans, what safeguards must exist before we give it irreversible power?

AUTHOR'S VIEW

That is the cautious position. But if you ask me, we should be panicking!

Superintelligence could potentially wipe us out as early as next year—or subject conscious beings to unimaginable, everlasting suffering.

And by everlasting, I mean suffering created by a digital god whose capabilities—and the horrors it could devise—are completely beyond our understanding.

Even if it grants us paradise, that paradise would ultimately be beyond our control—and could, at any moment, become something far worse: viz., the horrors that we cannot even begin to imagine.
